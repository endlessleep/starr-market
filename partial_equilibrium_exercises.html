Exercise 1

Exercise 1. Partial Equilibrium — Cobb-Douglas

Consider the production function:

\[f(z_1,z_2)=z_1^\alpha z_2^{1-\alpha},\qquad0<\alpha<1.\]

Short run

Input \(z_1\) is variable; \(z_2=\bar z_2>0\) is fixed. The input prices are \(w_1>0\) and \(\bar w_2>0\); output sells at \(p>0\).

  1. Find the optimal input \(z_1^*\) and the profit function in terms of \(p,\alpha,w_1,\bar w_2,\bar z_2\).
    Hint: \(\alpha^{1/(1-\alpha)}=\alpha\,\alpha^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\).
  2. Find individual supply using Hotelling's lemma.
  3. There are 48 identical firms, with \(\alpha=1/2\), \(w_1=4\), \(\bar w_2=1\), and \(\bar z_2=1\). Find market supply.
  4. Market demand is \(Q^d(p)=294/p\). Find the equilibrium price and market quantity.
  5. Compute each firm's equilibrium profit.
  6. Illustrate the market equilibrium in a diagram.

Long run

  1. With \(\alpha=1/2\), \(w_1=4\), \(w_2=1\), find the equilibrium price and the number of firms under free entry. Use the stated firm-size normalization \(\widehat z_2=1\), and distinguish this from unrestricted firm size.

For a CRS Cobb-Douglas technology, the unit cost is:

\[c(w)=\frac{w_1^\alpha w_2^{1-\alpha}}{\alpha^\alpha(1-\alpha)^{1-\alpha}}.\]

Optimal variable input and profit

1. Profit maximization

\[\max_{z_1\geq0}\;p z_1^\alpha\bar z_2^{1-\alpha}-w_1z_1-\bar w_2\bar z_2.\]

The fixed-input cost does not enter the first-order condition:

\[\alpha p z_1^{\alpha-1}\bar z_2^{1-\alpha}=w_1.\]
\[\boxed{z_1^*=\left(\frac{\alpha p}{w_1}\right)^{1/(1-\alpha)}\bar z_2.}\]

The second derivative confirms that this is the unique maximum:

\[\frac{\partial^2\pi}{\partial z_1^2}=\alpha(\alpha-1)p z_1^{\alpha-2}\bar z_2^{1-\alpha}<0.\]

2. Simplify profit using the first-order condition

Multiply the condition by \(z_1^*\): variable input expenditure is \(w_1z_1^*=\alpha p q^s\). Hence:

\[\pi^*=(1-\alpha)pq^s-\bar w_2\bar z_2.\]

Substituting the optimal input into production gives the profit function:

\[ \begin{aligned} \pi(p)={}&(1-\alpha)p^{1/(1-\alpha)}\\ &\times\left(\frac{\alpha}{w_1}\right)^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\bar z_2\\ &-\bar w_2\bar z_2. \end{aligned} \]

The fixed-input cost affects profit, but not the optimal variable input.

Individual supply — Hotelling’s lemma

Differentiate maximized profit with respect to the output price:

\[\begin{aligned} q^s(p)&=\frac{\partial\pi(p)}{\partial p}\\ &=p^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)} \left(\frac{\alpha}{w_1}\right)^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\bar z_2. \end{aligned}\]

The factor \(1-\alpha\) cancels the exponent \(1/(1-\alpha)\) brought down by differentiation.

\[\boxed{q^s(p)=\left(\frac{\alpha p}{w_1}\right)^{\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\bar z_2.}\]

This is also \(f(z_1^*,\bar z_2)\), a direct check of Hotelling's lemma.

Market supply with 48 firms

Here \(\alpha/(1-\alpha)=1\). Each firm's supply is:

\[q_j^s(p)=\left(\frac{p/2}{4}\right)\times1=\frac p8.\]

Sum the 48 identical supplies:

\[\boxed{Q^s(p)=48\frac p8=6p.}\]

Short-run equilibrium

\[\begin{aligned} Q^s(p)&=Q^d(p)\\ 6p&=\frac{294}{p}\\ p^2&=49. \end{aligned}\]

Keep the positive price:

\[\boxed{p^*=7},\qquad\boxed{Q^*=42}.\]

Each firm produces \(q_j^*=42/48=7/8\). Use \(Q\) for total market output and \(q_j\) for one firm's output.

Equilibrium profit per firm

With the numerical parameters, Question 1 gives:

\[\pi_j(p)=\frac{p^2}{16}-1.\]
\[\boxed{\pi_j^*=\frac{49}{16}-1=\frac{33}{16}=2.0625.}\]

Check by subtracting input costs from revenue:

\[z_{1j}^*=\left(\frac78\right)^2=\frac{49}{64}.\]
\[\pi_j^*=7\frac78-4\frac{49}{64}-1=\frac{33}{16}.\]

Market equilibrium diagram

Put price on the vertical axis

Invert the market functions before plotting:

\[\begin{gathered} p=\frac Q6\quad\text{(supply)},\\ p=\frac{294}{Q}\quad\text{(demand)}. \end{gathered}\]

Producer surplus and profit are different

\[PS=\frac12\times42\times7=147.\]

The 48 firms each pay fixed cost \(\bar w_2\bar z_2=1\). Therefore:

\[\boxed{\sum_{j=1}^{48}\pi_j=147-48=99.}\]

This agrees with \(48\times33/16=99\). Labeling the blue area as total profit would omit fixed costs.

Long-run price, output, and firm size

1. Constant returns give constant unit cost

\[\widehat p=c=\frac{\sqrt4\sqrt1}{\sqrt{1/2}\sqrt{1/2}}=4.\]

At \(p<c\), firms do not produce; at \(p>c\), CRS firms would want to expand without bound. Positive finite output is compatible with free-entry equilibrium at \(p=c\), with zero profit.

\[\boxed{\widehat p=4},\qquad\boxed{\widehat Q=\frac{294}{4}=73.5}.\]

2. Use the stated normalization \(\widehat z_2=1\)

Cost minimization equates the marginal-product ratio to the input-price ratio:

\[\frac{MP_1}{MP_2}=\frac{z_2}{z_1}=\frac{w_1}{w_2}=4.\]

Thus \(\widehat z_1=1/4\), and each normalized firm produces:

\[\widehat q_j=\sqrt{\frac14\times1}=\frac12.\]
\[\boxed{\widehat N=\frac{\widehat Q}{\widehat q_j}=\frac{73.5}{0.5}=147.}\]

Profit is \(4(1/2)-4(1/4)-1=0\). Positive short-run profit attracts entry: the normalized number of firms rises from 48 to 147.

Firm size matters. With unrestricted scale under CRS, \(\widehat p=4\) and \(\widehat Q=73.5\) are determined, but the number of firms is not: firms can split total output in many ways. The value 147 uses \(\widehat z_2=1\) for every firm. Rounding market output 73.5 to obtain 75 firms is not justified.
Exercise 2

Exercise 2. Partial Equilibrium — Entry Costs

Good \(x\) is sold at price \(p\) per unit. There are 15 consumers: ten have income \(m_i=5\), and five have income \(m_i=10\). Each has utility \(u_i(x)=4x\).

There are initially 9 identical firms with technology:

\[f_j(z_1,z_2)=(z_1-1)^{1/3}(z_2-1)^{1/3}.\]

Input prices are \(w_1,w_2>0\). Positive production requires \(z_1,z_2>1\). Assume the short-run equilibrium price makes production profitable for all nine firms.

Short run here means a fixed number of firms. Both inputs are variable. In the long run, firms can enter or exit.

Short run

  1. Find market demand.
  2. Find market supply: first minimize each firm's costs, then maximize profit and apply Hotelling's lemma.
  3. Find the short-run equilibrium price.

Long run

  1. Find individual supply functions. Hint: find minimum average cost.
  2. Find the equilibrium price and the number of firms. Hint: \(p=AC(q)\) at a free-entry equilibrium.

Market demand

Utility is increasing in \(x\), so each consumer spends all income on the only good:

\[px_i=m_i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_i^*=\frac{m_i}{p}.\]
\[\boxed{Q^d(p)=10\frac5p+5\frac{10}{p}=\frac{100}{p}.}\]

The coefficient 4 in utility does not change demand: it does not change which affordable quantity is best.

From input choices to market supply

1. Cost minimization for \(q>0\)

\[\begin{gathered} \min_{z_1,z_2>1}\;w_1z_1+w_2z_2\\ \text{s.t.}\quad(z_1-1)(z_2-1)=q^3. \end{gathered}\]

Using the logarithm of the production constraint:

\[\begin{aligned} \mathcal L={}&w_1z_1+w_2z_2\\ &+\lambda\left[3\ln q-\ln(z_1-1)-\ln(z_2-1)\right]. \end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{gathered} w_1-\frac{\lambda}{z_1-1}=0,\qquad w_2-\frac{\lambda}{z_2-1}=0,\\ \frac{z_1-1}{z_2-1}=\frac{w_2}{w_1}. \end{gathered}\]

Substitute the input ratio into the production constraint:

\[q^3=\frac{w_2}{w_1}(z_2-1)^2.\]
\[\begin{gathered} \boxed{z_1^c(q)=1+\sqrt{\frac{w_2}{w_1}}q^{3/2}},\\ \boxed{z_2^c(q)=1+\sqrt{\frac{w_1}{w_2}}q^{3/2}}. \end{gathered}\]

2. Minimized total cost

Multiply the input demands by their prices and add:

\[\boxed{C(q)=w_1+w_2+2\sqrt{w_1w_2}\,q^{3/2}.}\]

Write \(F=w_1+w_2\) and \(A=\sqrt{w_1w_2}\). Then \(C(q)=F+2Aq^{3/2}\) for positive output. The term \(F\) is the cost of the baseline input units needed to operate, even though both inputs are variable.

3. Profit-maximizing output

\[\pi(q)=pq-F-2Aq^{3/2}.\]
\[\pi'(q)=p-3A\sqrt q=0.\]
\[\boxed{q_j^s(p)=\frac{p^2}{9w_1w_2}.}\]

This is the unique optimum conditional on producing, since:

\[\pi''(q)=-\frac{3A}{2\sqrt q}<0.\]

Substitute optimal output into profit:

\[\begin{aligned} \pi_j^*(p)&=\frac{p^3}{9w_1w_2}-F-\frac{2p^3}{27w_1w_2}\\ &=\boxed{\frac{p^3}{27w_1w_2}-(w_1+w_2)}. \end{aligned}\]

Both baseline input costs are subtracted. The active branch applies because the exercise assumes all nine firms find production profitable.

4. Hotelling's lemma and aggregation

\[\frac{\partial\pi_j^*(p)}{\partial p}=\frac{p^2}{9w_1w_2}=q_j^s(p).\]
\[\boxed{Q^s(p)=9q_j^s(p)=\frac{p^2}{w_1w_2}.}\]

Short-run equilibrium price

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{p^2}{w_1w_2}&=\frac{100}{p}\\ p^3&=100w_1w_2. \end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{p_{SR}=(100w_1w_2)^{1/3}}.\]
\[Q_{SR}=\frac{100}{p_{SR}},\qquad q_{j,SR}=\frac{Q_{SR}}9.\]
Check the assumption: equilibrium profit is \(\pi_{j,SR}=100/27-(w_1+w_2)\). Strictly profitable production by all nine firms requires \(w_1+w_2<100/27\).

Minimum average cost and individual supply

1. Find the efficient operating scale

Using \(F=w_1+w_2\) and \(A=\sqrt{w_1w_2}\):

\[AC(q)=\frac Fq+2A\sqrt q.\]
\[\begin{aligned} AC'(q)&=-\frac F{q^2}+\frac A{\sqrt q}=0\\ Aq^{3/2}&=F. \end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{q_{LR}=\left(\frac{w_1+w_2}{\sqrt{w_1w_2}}\right)^{2/3}}.\]

Since \(AC'(q)=(Aq^{3/2}-F)/q^2\), average cost decreases before this output and increases after it: this is its unique minimum.

\[AC''(q_{LR})=\frac{3F}{2q_{LR}^3}>0.\]

2. Compare producing with staying out

A firm can avoid the operating cost by not entering. Let \(\bar p=\min AC=3(Fw_1w_2)^{1/3}\). Individual long-run supply is:

\[q_j^s(p)=\begin{cases} 0,&p<\bar p,\\ \{0,q_{LR}\},&p=\bar p,\\ \dfrac{p^2}{9w_1w_2},&p>\bar p. \end{cases}\]

At the break-even price, the firm is indifferent between staying out and producing \(q_{LR}\). Above it, positive profit attracts entry.

Long-run equilibrium price and number of firms

1. Price equals minimum average cost

At the minimum, \(F/q_{LR}=A\sqrt{q_{LR}}\). Therefore:

\[\begin{aligned} p_{LR}&=AC(q_{LR})\\ &=3A\sqrt{q_{LR}}\\ &=\boxed{3(w_1+w_2)^{1/3}(w_1w_2)^{1/3}}. \end{aligned}\]

This also equals \(MC(q_{LR})\), and each active firm earns zero profit.

2. Divide market demand by output per firm

\[Q_{LR}=\frac{100}{p_{LR}},\qquad N_{LR}=\frac{Q_{LR}}{q_{LR}}.\]

Use \(p_{LR}q_{LR}=3Aq_{LR}^{3/2}=3F\):

\[\boxed{N_{LR}=\frac{100}{3(w_1+w_2)}}.\]

Under the short-run strict-profit assumption, \(w_1+w_2<100/27\), so \(N_{LR}>9\): entry lowers price until profit is zero.

Integer firms: this is the usual continuous-number benchmark. With indivisible firms, an exact price-taking free-entry equilibrium of this form requires \(N_{LR}\) to be an integer. Arbitrary rounding generally breaks market clearing or the zero-profit condition.

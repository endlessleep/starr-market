What Use Is Microeconomic Theory?

Part I
Varian's Advocacy
Discussion Question
According to you, what use is economic theory?
📊

1. Substituting for Data

Helps predict outcomes when direct data are lacking.

When direct empirical data is missing or counterfactual scenarios cannot be directly observed, theory provides a structured framework built on logical assumptions. This allows economists to deduce predictions and evaluate economic outcomes without waiting for unavailable dataset numbers.

🎯

2. Guiding the Identification of Key Parameters

Helps in identifying which parameters are important for understanding economic phenomena.

Faced with complex real-world datasets, researchers often do not know which variables drive behavior. Microeconomic theory acts as an essential filter: it isolates the critical parameters (such as elasticities or substitution rates) that govern economic choices, focusing research on what truly matters.

🔗

3. Connecting Disparate Problems

Can reveal that seemingly different economic problems are related, providing a unifying framework.

Economic issues that appear unrelated on the surface — such as labor supply decisions, optimal tax policy, or auction design — often share the exact same underlying mathematical structure. Theory reveals this common ground, enabling insights from one domain to solve problems in another.

💡

4. Generating Useful Insights

Even if a theory is not entirely accurate, it can still provide valuable insights into economic mechanisms that are useful in policy debates.

Economic models are simplified representations of reality and do not need to be 100% realistic to be beneficial. By isolating fundamental economic mechanisms and trade-offs, even stylised models generate crucial qualitative insights that inform policy debates.

🧮

5. Offering Quantification and Calculation

Provides a framework for quantifying relationships, essential for informed policy decision-making.

Policy decisions require precise measurements, not just intuition. Microeconomic theory supplies the quantitative tools needed to calculate specific numerical values — such as welfare losses, tax incidence, or optimal subsidies — translating qualitative ideas into concrete calculations.

🧠

6. Serving as an Antidote to Introspection

Helps overcome biases that arise from personal experience and introspection, leading to a more objective understanding of economic issues.

Personal anecdotes and intuition are inherently biased by individual experience, which often misleads economic analysis. Theory imposes a strict logical discipline: by forcing economists to state explicit assumptions, it produces objective conclusions that transcend personal subjectivity.

🔬

7. Enabling Experimental Verification

Allows for the formulation of hypotheses that can be tested through experiments, refining theories and improving their real-world applicability.

For economics to be a rigorous science, its claims must be testable and falsifiable. Theoretical models produce precise hypotheses that can be confronted with experimental or empirical evidence, creating a feedback loop that continuously refines economic theory.

Part II
Rubinstein's Dilemmas
Discussion Question
Put yourself in the shoes of an economic theorist: what challenges do you face when presenting your work?
• Theory leads to absurd conclusions • Theory is rejected by the data • Empirics are enough to identify behavioral regularities • Theory is not relevant to the real-world issues
1
The Dilemma of Absurd Conclusions
❓ Question / Challenge

Should a model be discarded if it leads to absurd conclusions, or should it be seen as a limited tool that fails in some contexts?

✔ Slide Resolution / Answer

Economists should recognize that all models are simplifications of reality and will inevitably fail in some contexts. These absurdities highlight the limitations of the model's assumptions and serve as an opportunity to reinterpret them.

An absurd conclusion signals that an assumption has been stretched past its boundary of validity — serving as a diagnostic tool to refine the model's domain rather than abandoning it entirely.

2
The Dilemma of Responding to Evidence
❓ Question / Challenge

Should economic models be adjusted or rejected if they are contradicted by experimental evidence?

✔ Slide Resolution / Answer

Economists should use empirical refutations as a basis for refining models or developing new ones.

Empirical failure is not a defeat; it provides concrete evidence of where a model's mechanics break down, pointing theorists directly toward necessary revisions or new modeling frameworks.

3
The Dilemma of Modelless Regularities
❓ Question / Challenge

Should economic theorists rely on models to discover regularities in human behavior, or can these regularities be identified directly through observation?

✔ Slide Resolution / Answer

Regularities can often be observed directly without the need for models. However, models can still play a useful role by providing a structured way to think about these regularities.

Observing a pattern in data is distinct from understanding its cause. Formal theory provides the analytical structure needed to explain why regularities exist and whether they will hold across new environments.

4
The Dilemma of Relevance
❓ Question / Challenge

Can economic theorists offer meaningful advice that impacts the real world, or are their models too detached from reality to be relevant?

✔ Slide Resolution / Answer

While economic theory may not always provide actionable advice, it is still relevant because it shapes the way people think about economic issues. Theorists should be responsible in how they present their work, recognizing the limitations of their models and avoiding oversimplification when addressing complex real-world problems.

Even when models do not yield direct policy prescriptions, they define the language and conceptual frameworks decision-makers use. Theorists bear a responsibility to state model limitations clearly.

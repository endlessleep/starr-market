2. Research Focus

Evidence on IT, cloud computing, and robot adoption.

Research focus

Research Focus: IT, Robots and Cloud Technologies

This research focus studies the consequences of declines in IT prices, cloud-services adoption, and robot adoption for firms.

Falling IT prices

Lashkari, Bauer, & Boussard (2024), Information technology and returns to scale.

Context and questions

IT prices have declined substantially over recent decades. The study asks how these declines affect returns to scale at the firm level, firms’ concentration, and the labor share.

Data and method

Firm-level data from France, 1989–2007. The analysis estimates firm-level elasticity of IT demand.

Two possible effects

  • Substitution effect: firms substitute IT for other inputs as IT becomes cheaper.
  • Returns-to-scale effect: as output grows, demand for IT rises more than proportionally; cheaper IT can therefore generate increasing returns to scale.

Results

  • IT demand is scale-dependent. Larger firms invest more in IT per employee: doubling firm size is associated with a 30–50% increase in IT investment per employee.
  • Returns to scale. Larger firms have higher IT-demand elasticity as output increases. IT-price declines lead to increasing returns to scale for larger firms.
  • Concentration and labor share. IT-price declines raise profit shares at IT-intensive firms because of higher returns to scale. They contribute to concentration in production at larger, more IT-intensive firms. IT-intensive firms reduce labor’s share of income, contributing to an aggregate decline in labor share.

Policy implications

Larger, IT-heavy firms benefit most from lower IT prices, which increases productivity disparities. Smaller firms may need policy support to adopt IT and remain competitive.

Cloud services’ adoption

Context and questions

Cloud computing transforms traditional IT infrastructure into a pay-on-demand service. The study asks how cloud adoption affects firm growth and performance, and how it changes the geographical distribution of firm activities.

Data

Firm-level data on more than 15,000 UK firms, 2013–2015.

Methods

  • Fiber broadband speeds are used as an instrument for cloud adoption.
  • A difference-in-difference approach compares firms according to proximity to fiber-enabled broadband; firms closer to it are more likely to adopt cloud computing.

Results

  • Firm performance. Young firms, under 10 years old, experience significant annual increases in employment (+14.3%) and sales (+15.9%). Incumbent firms, over 10 years old, show no significant effect on employment or sales. Productivity gains are higher for younger firms.
  • Geographical distribution. Employees work further away from headquarters. There is less geographical dispersion: younger firms are less likely to become multi-centric, while incumbent firms are more likely to close some centers.

Implications

Cloud computing helps young firms grow in size and productivity, while incumbent firms focus on restructuring. Cloud technologies may reduce barriers for new entrants and level the playing field between small and large firms.

Robots’ adoption

Koch, Manuylov, & Smolka (2021), Robots and firms.

Context and questions

The study responds to concerns about robots replacing human labor. It asks which firm characteristics make robot adoption more likely and how adoption affects firm performance.

Data and method

Firm-level data on more than 5,500 firms in Spain, 1990–2016. The method combines difference-in-difference estimation with propensity-score matching.

Firms adopting robots

  • Larger, more productive firms and exporters are more likely to adopt robots.
  • Conditional on size, firms with higher skill intensity are less likely to adopt.

Impact on firm performance

  • Robot adoption raises output by 20–25% within four years.
  • Adopting firms experience a 10% increase in total employment, including low- and high-skilled jobs.
  • The labor-cost share falls by 5–7 percentage points after adoption.

Implications

Encouraging robot adoption may raise productivity, but it also requires attention to inequalities between adopting and non-adopting firms.
